Pan-Seared Salmon with Garlic Butter

Salt, pepper, garlic, and the discipline to leave the fish alone.

Serves4
Stovemedium-high, then medium
Timeabout 15 minutes

This is the supper I make when I don't feel like making supper. Salt, pepper, garlic — nothing you don't already have — and fifteen minutes from cold pan to table. The whole recipe is really one instruction wearing different clothes: leave the fish alone. Dry it well, lay it in the hot oil, and then keep your hands off. It will stick, and then, when the crust is ready, it will let go. The fish decides, not you.

The garlic butter at the end is the part that makes it feel like more than it was. Butter, smashed cloves, a few sprigs of thyme, tipped and spooned over the fish until the kitchen smells like a restaurant. Nobody needs to know it took three minutes.

Ingredients

The fish

  • 4 salmon fillets, about 6 oz each
  • Kosher salt and coarsely ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 2 Tbsp olive oil

The garlic butter

  • 3 Tbsp unsalted butter
  • 3 garlic cloves, smashed
  • A few sprigs of fresh thyme
  • ½ lemon, plus more for serving

Garlic powder in the seasoning, fresh garlic in the butter. Fresh cloves burn during a hard sear and turn bitter — the powder carries the crust, and the smashed cloves perfume the baste.

Method
  1. Pat the fillets very dry — drier than seems necessary. Season generously on all sides with salt, pepper, and the garlic powder, and let them sit 10 minutes while the pan heats.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high until it shimmers.
  3. Lay the fillets in flesh-side down and press gently for a few seconds so they make full contact with the pan. Then leave them alone — a full 4 minutes, no peeking — until a deep golden crust releases on its own.
  4. Flip the fillets, lower the heat to medium, and add the butter, smashed garlic, and thyme to the pan.
  5. Tilt the pan and spoon the foaming garlic butter over the fish for 2–3 minutes, until the center is just barely opaque.
  6. Rest the fillets a few minutes off the heat, then squeeze the lemon over and serve with more alongside.

If your fillets are skin-on, start skin-side down instead and give it an extra minute — the skin crisps into the best part.

"The fish tells you when it's ready — it stops holding onto the pan. Most of cooking is learning to wait for that."
